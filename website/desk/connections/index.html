A developer has asked about connecting a 100 MW AI factory in northern Tasmania. Before anyone runs detailed studies, screen the enquiry against what TasNetworks has published, and write a short note: can it connect as asked, and what are the issues? This is practice: the enquiry is made up; the figures are TasNetworks' published ones.

1 Learn

How a connection happens

A customer makes an enquiry; the network business gives a preliminary response; then come an application, an offer and a connection agreement. The commercial terms are negotiated, but the technical terms are prescribed in the National Electricity Rules.

NEM course p. 85 · stages: [ASSUMED] general description of the Rules, chapter 5

MW and MVA

MW is real power, what does the work. MVA is apparent power, what the equipment actually has to carry. MVA = MW ÷ power factor, so a 100 MW load at power factor 0.95 needs about 105 MVA of capacity. Networks rate their transformers in MVA.

Textbook 03-08, 03-09

Firm and non-firm

Firm capacity can still supply the load after a single contingency, the loss of one element. A non-firm connection may be interrupted when that happens. Table 4-1 publishes each zone substation's firm spare capacity ("headroom").

Report pp. 62–63, 108–109

One failure must not take too much

No more than 850 MW of load may be interrupted by a single asset failure. At George Town, 300 MW or more of new load could at times be 60% of the State's load; losing that substation would then be a system black. Southern demand is forecast near the 850 MW limit.

Report pp. 52, 56, 114

System strength and AI factories

An AI factory draws power through electronics, so it's an "inverter-based" load. New access standards for large inverter-based loads were finalised in 2026, and system strength margins differ around the island: low at George Town, positive at Burnie.

Report pp. 5, 23, 80–90

Responsive loads

A load that can rapidly reduce its demand after a fault helps keep the system secure, and the report says that means less need for new transmission lines. It's one of the first questions to ask a developer.

Report p. 52

What this desk shows, and what it doesn't. It shows how to read a connection enquiry against published planning figures and rules, and how to write the note. It doesn't run any power system study: no load flow, fault level or stability analysis. Network planners do those in specialist software such as PSS/E or PowerFactory [ASSUMED: general industry practice]. This is not an assessment by TasNetworks.
