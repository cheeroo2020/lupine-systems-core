Scene 02 · The stack

Where everyone stops

Five layers sit between a person clicking "pay" and money actually moving.

Most fintechs build at the API layer — the comfortable one, with REST endpoints and JSON.

The power sits one floor below, where the instructions live.

Scene 03 · Why it matters

A message is an instruction

A SWIFT message isn't a description of a payment that happened somewhere else. It is the thing that makes it happen. Every downstream system reads it and acts.

One MT103, inboundthe instruction

:20: LUPINE0500K :32A: 260727AUD500000, :57A: DBSSSGSG :59: /SG BENEFICIARY ACCOUNT

What it triggers

→ Sanctions engine screens the parties → Treasury reserves nostro liquidity → RTGS queues a settlement instruction → Correspondent books a ledger entry → Compliance opens a case file

Five systems, five different institutions, all acting on one block of text. If the text is ambiguous, they act differently.

Scene 06 · Generation three

gpi adds tracking

SWIFT gpi is an overlay, not a replacement. It bolts end-to-end tracking onto the existing rails.

You get hop-level timestamps and fee transparency. Genuinely useful — the industry had nothing before it.

But look closely at when each timestamp arrives.

gpi tracker · UETR a3f9…hop log

08:00 · AU · sent 08:12 · C1 · credited 08:31 · C2 · credited 09:42 · C3 · credited — 90 min delay     reason: liquidity

Accurate. Complete. Delivered at 09:42.

Scene 07 · The key distinction

Rear-view mirror vs windshield

Same 90-minute liquidity stall at the Singapore correspondent

With SWIFT gpi 08:00
↑ 09:42 · "delayed 90 min"

You find out at 09:42. The delay already happened.

With Aiva 08:00
↓ 08:00 · SG liquidity thin — reroute now

You know at 08:00. The delay never happens.

gpi tracks problems. It does not prevent them. It can't predict the shortage, can't reroute around it, can't score whether another corridor would have been faster.

Scene 08 · The Lupine move

ISO 20022 as a semantic graph

Banks adopt ISO 20022 for migration compliance and data quality. Lupine reads the same structured fields as intelligence input. In Aiva's model, one payment is six things simultaneously:

Constraint set Time windows, limits, minimums it must satisfy
Risk vector Failure probability across every candidate hop
Compliance object Jurisdictions, parties, purpose codes to pre-screen
Corridor node A point on the FX connectivity graph
Time-window object Which RTGS sessions it can still catch
Liquidity event A demand shock on someone's nostro pool

None of this is readable from free text. All of it is readable from schema-validated fields.

Scene 10 · The thesis

Visibility → intelligence

What gpi gave the industry

  • Hop-level timestamps
  • Fee transparency
  • Proof of where it got stuck
  • All of it, after the fact

What it still can't do

  • Predict a liquidity shortage
  • Reroute around a compliance block
  • Score whether another corridor is faster
  • Act before the money moves
"Most fintechs build on the API layer. The real control sits one level down, in the messaging layer — those messages are legal instructions telling every system in the chain what to do. gpi gave the industry visibility. It didn't give it intelligence."

Scene 11 · Check yourself

Say these out loud

You answered two of these correctly back in April. Prove they're still there.

Why is gpi "visibility, not intelligence"?Nailed it in April

It timestamps problems after they happen. It can't predict liquidity shortages, prevent compliance blocks, reroute, or score alternatives. A rear-view mirror shows you what you've already driven past.

Why can't you build cryptographic evidence from MT103?Correct in April

Free-text fields with no programmatic separation — names, addresses and purpose codes run together with no defined boundary. To hash a field you must know exactly where it begins and ends. You can't hash ambiguity.

What do banks use ISO 20022 for, versus what Lupine uses it for?

Banks: migration compliance and data quality — cleaner records, easier reporting. Lupine: a semantic graph feeding corridor scoring, routing optimisation, failure prediction, FX exposure modelling, compliance pre-checks and evidence construction. Same data, completely different purpose.
