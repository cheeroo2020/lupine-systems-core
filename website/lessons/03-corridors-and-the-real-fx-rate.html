Scene 02 · What a corridor is

Same payment, three paths

A corridor is a pathway between two currencies — geopolitical, regulatory, liquidity-dependent. The same payment can take completely different corridors with wildly different outcomes.

A$500,000 · AUD → EURall-in cost

AUD → SGD → EUR Via Singapore. Deep regional pool, stable, good window fit. 41 bps
AUD → USD → EUR Via the dollar. Deepest liquidity, but an extra hop and spread. 58 bps
AUD → EUR direct Looks shortest. Thin liquidity means a punishing spread. 94 bps

The shortest path on a map is the most expensive one in practice.

Scene 03 · Where liquidity lives

Three tiers

Liquidity isn't evenly spread. It pools in tiers, and which tier your corridor touches decides your spread.

Above it all sit macro conditions — geopolitics, interest rates — which move the whole structure at once.

Scene 06 · Mechanism #2

Exposure = σ × √t

Hold money mid-flight and the rate can move against you. That risk is volatility × the square root of settlement time.

The √ is the whole insight. Exposure grows non-linearly — so the first hours of delay hurt far more than the last ones.

Quadruple the time and you only double the damage.

Scene 07 · The consequence

Fastest ≠ cheapest

Route A arrives 6 hours earlier and costs A$1,150 more. Its volatility and liquidity premiums outweigh everything it saved on time.

Aiva isn't picking the fastest route or the cheapest route. It's picking the route with the lowest total exposure.

Scene 08 · Why delay compounds

D feeds V feeds ΔS

The eight components aren't independent. Three of them form a loop, and it only ever turns one way.

D · delay premium The payment sits longer than planned — a queue, a missed window, a compliance hold.
→ V · volatility premium More time in flight means more exposure to price movement. σ√t rises.
→ ΔS · spread Liquidity providers price that added risk in. The spread widens on the same trade.

Slowness isn't merely inconvenient. It is literally more expensive.

This is why latency and cost can't be scored separately — and why Aiva treats FX cost and exposure risk as a pair.

Scene 09 · Three different questions

Who optimises for what

Banks

"What's cheapest for us?"

Locked into existing correspondent relationships and pre-funded nostro accounts. They route on treasury cost optimisation — their balance sheet, not your outcome.

Fintechs

"How do we skip the middlemen?"

Avoid correspondents entirely — netting, internal ledgers, local rails. Genuinely better, but only across their own network.

Lupine

"What's best for this payment?"

No balance sheet to protect and no network to defend. Evaluates every available path and optimises for the payment's outcome.

The neutrality isn't a feature bolted on. It's the only position from which the question can be asked honestly.

Scene 10 · The thesis

Corridor intelligence

What the market shows you

  • S₀ — the interbank spot rate
  • ΔS — some of the spread
  • Two components out of eight
  • No pricing of delay at all

What Aiva models

  • All eight components, per corridor
  • Exposure as σ√t, summed across hops
  • Delay priced as a real cost
  • Six scoring dimensions, not one number
"Everyone thinks the FX rate is one number. It's eight stacked components, and banks only show you two. We model all eight — including the one nobody prices properly: the cost of delay itself."
