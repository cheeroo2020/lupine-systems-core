Scene 10 · The thesis

Reactive → predictive

Legacy · reactive

  • Send the payment, then deal with failures
  • Routing set by agreements made years ago
  • Each institution decides blind to the others
  • You learn about the delay after it happens
  • Two identical payments, two different paths

Lupine · predictive

  • Evaluate every route before the money moves
  • Routing computed per payment, per moment
  • All constraints weighed simultaneously
  • The outcome is knowable in advance
  • Same inputs, same route, every time
"A cross-border payment isn't one transaction — it's a relay through four institutions that don't share hours, rules, or software. Legacy systems send it and deal with failures afterwards. Lupine evaluates the routes, predicts the failures, and picks the best path before the money moves."

Scene 11 · Check yourself

Say these out loud

Answer before you open them. Speaking is the test — reading feels like learning and isn't.

Why can the same A$500K payment take 2.5 hours or 11 days?

Different routes, different correspondents, different settlement windows, different compliance interpretations at each hop. Nothing precomputes the path — it follows static agreements set years ago, so the outcome is discovered rather than chosen.

What is a nostro account, and what happens when it runs dry?

"Our money held with them" — a pre-funded account your bank holds at a foreign bank. When it's exhausted the payments don't bounce; they queue invisibly until it's topped up.

If SWIFT doesn't move money — what does?

RTGS systems (RITS, TARGET2, Fedwire), nostro/vostro balances, FX desks and clearing houses. SWIFT only carries the instructions telling those systems what to do.
