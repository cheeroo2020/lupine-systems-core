Why every comparable flow in the economy became predictable, and value movement did not.

1.

If you send A$500,000 from Sydney to Singapore today, the cost is somewhere between 45 and 250 basis points and the money arrives somewhere between two and a half hours and eleven days later. It is the same payment either way, however the outcome is based on a routing decision that nobody actually computed.

2.

The path follows correspondent agreements that were signed years before the money existed, and the sending bank cannot tell you in advance which outcome you are going to get, because the information required to answer does not exist in any single place.

1.1Four flows that stopped guessing

3.

Modern infrastructure has mostly moved in one direction, which is away from reactive management and towards computed prediction.

4.

Physical goods moved first. Before containerisation, loading a cargo ship was manual and bespoke and took days in port, at roughly $5.86 per ton, whereas arrival dates were estimates rather than commitments. The insight in 1956 was not a better crane, it was a standard unit — once every box was the same box, the whole chain could be planned in advance. Cost fell towards $0.16 per ton, however the more important change was that schedules became commitments.

5.

Data followed. Early databases lost records on power failure as a matter of routine, because a crash during a write left the data half updated with no way of knowing which half. The fix was the transaction, which guarantees that a write either commits fully or rolls back fully, whereas nothing in between is permitted.

6.

Communications solved it differently. Packet networks drop packets and reorder them and duplicate them, however TCP did not attempt to stop that happening. It made the unreliability irrelevant to the application, based on sequence numbers, acknowledgements and retransmission, so the network stayed messy whereas the interface became certain.

7.

Compute was last. Before 2006, capacity meant procurement — quotes, purchase orders, racking, several weeks of lead time and a guess about demand. Afterwards it meant an API call and a service level agreement. The underlying resource did not change, however the predictability of obtaining it did.

The pattern

A flow stops being managed reactively and starts being computed in advance, and the guarantee moves into the infrastructure instead of staying with the user.
