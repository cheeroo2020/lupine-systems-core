1.1The observation

If you send A$500,000 from Sydney to Singapore today, the cost is somewhere between 45 and 250 bps and the money arrives somewhere between 2.5 hours and 11 days later.1 It is the same payment in both cases – the difference is based on a routing decision that nobody computed.

01 · not established Source gives these bands for AU→SG→DE, a three-leg corridor. Restating them for AU→SG is unsupported. Needs primary data.

As per published fee schedules across 21 providers on this corridor, the quoted rate accounts for approximately 12 bps of the total cost, whereas the actual cost is approximately 54 bps.2 The remaining 42 bps is not disclosed at any point, which suggests the payer is not in a position to evaluate the transaction they are agreeing to.

02 · not established Source states the eight-component formula but gives no values. These bps figures are illustrative and were not measured.

The sending bank cannot resolve this in advance either. The information required to answer sits across four institutions that do not share operating hours, liquidity positions or compliance interpretations, however no single participant is responsible for assembling it.3

1.2Comparable flows

Four other flows solved the same problem, and in each case the mechanism was different however the pattern was the same.

Physical goods – as per the standard estimates, break-bulk loading cost approximately $5.86 per ton and took days in port, whereas containerised loading brought this to approximately $0.16 per ton.4 The change in 1956 was not equipment, it was standardisation of the unit, which meant the chain could be planned in advance.

04 · unchecked Widely cited figures, quoted from recall. No primary source consulted.

Data – early databases lost records on power failure as a matter of routine, based on the fact that a crash during a write left the record half updated with no way of identifying which half. The transaction resolved this by guaranteeing that a write either commits fully or rolls back fully, whereas nothing in between is permitted.5

Communications – packet networks drop, reorder and duplicate packets, however TCP did not attempt to prevent this. It made the unreliability irrelevant to the application, based on sequence numbers, acknowledgements and retransmission.6

05 · general · 06 · verified ACID is textbook, no citation given. TCP retransmission is RFC 793.

Compute – before 2006 capacity meant procurement, which is quotes, purchase orders, racking and several weeks of lead time. Afterwards it meant an API call and an SLA. The resource did not change, however the predictability of obtaining it did.7

The pattern in all four is the same: the flow stops being managed reactively and starts being computed in advance, and the guarantee moves into the infrastructure instead of staying with the user.

07 · corrected EC2 was public beta in 2006. General availability was 2008. Text still says otherwise.

1.3The measurement

A cloud API call taking 106× its median latency would be treated as a severity-one incident, whereas in cross-border value movement that spread is the normal operating envelope.8
