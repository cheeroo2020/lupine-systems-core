## Summary

Ten modules, about 3 hours of reading plus videos and activities. The course explains the NEM both as a
**physical power system** and as a **wholesale market** (p. 1). For an office-based role at a network
business, modules 2, 3, 4, 6 and 10 matter most. Modules 8 and 9 (settlements, financial markets) are for
understanding, not specialising.

## What the source says: the essentials

### Who does what (module 2)
- **The law:** the National Electricity Law is the highest authority. The National Electricity Rules have the force of law (pp. 43–44).
- **The five bodies** (p. 46):
  - ECMC sets policy;
  - **AEMC** makes the rules;
  - **AER** regulates and enforces;
  - **AEMO** operates the system and the market;
  - the EAP advises.
- The National Electricity Objective is efficient investment and operation "for the long-term interest of consumers" (p. 47).

### Networks (module 3)
- **Who owns the networks:** network businesses own, operate and control the network. Tasmania's transmission and distribution business is TasNetworks (p. 84).
- **Basslink** "has transitioned to a regulated TNSP" from 1 July 2026 (p. 84).
- **How networks are paid:**
  - networks are natural monopolies;
  - the AER sets their maximum allowable revenue, usually every five years (p. 86);
  - customers pay through transmission and distribution use-of-system charges (p. 87).
- **Losses** are about 10% overall, so delivering 100 MW needs about 110 MW sent out (p. 88).
- **Security** means staying within technical limits if one element disconnects (p. 91).
- **Planning:** a regulatory investment test (RIT-T) is needed for transmission upgrades, and it must consider non-network options (pp. 93–94).

### The power system (module 4)
- **Balance:** supply and demand must match every second. AEMO's two control centres run around the clock (pp. 100–101).
- **Normal frequency band:** 49.85–50.15 Hz (p. 114).
- **Credible contingency:** an event reasonably possible now, usually the loss of one element (pp. 102, 106).
- **Reserves:** should cover losing the two largest generating units in a region. The "lack of reserve" levels run LOR1 to LOR3 (pp. 110–112).

### Dispatch and prices (module 5)
- **Dispatch:** every five minutes, by least-cost optimisation over generators' bids (pp. 129–130).
- **Regions:** five, each with a reference node where its price is set. Interconnector limits cause price differences between regions (pp. 123, 133–137).
- **Price limits** in 2025–26 (p. 132):
  - market price cap: $20,300 (per MWh);
  - market floor price: −$1,000/MWh.

### Ancillary services (module 6)
- **Three kinds** (pp. 149–150):
  - frequency control (FCAS);
  - network support (NSCAS);
  - system restart (SRAS).
- **FCAS** has four speeds: very fast (1 s, added October 2023), fast (6 s), slow (60 s) and delayed (5 min) (pp. 156–157).
- **Local FCAS** is needed when a region is islanded (p. 159). This matters for Tasmania whenever Basslink is out.

### Where to find more (module 10)
- **AEMO owns** no generation or transmission assets (p. 273).
- **Planning documents** (pp. 274–277):
  - the Integrated System Plan, every two years, looking 20 years ahead;
  - the Electricity Statement of Opportunities, 10 years;
  - Quarterly Energy Dynamics.
- **Data:** NEMWeb gives public data downloads (p. 281).

## What the course doesn't cover

- **Inertia** isn't covered at all, and **system strength** appears only once (p. 164). Both are central in Tasmania: learn them from TasNetworks' planning report (pp. 80–92) and textbook 03-19, 03-20 and 05-22.
- **Hydro Tasmania and Marinus Link** aren't named.

## Our interpretation

The course gives the vocabulary and the map. For a network-business interview, the strongest preparation
pairs each module with TasNetworks' own report:
- module 3 with the report's chapters 3 and 4;
- module 4 and module 6 with chapter 5;
- module 2 with appendix A.
